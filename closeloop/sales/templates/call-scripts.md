# Call scripts

## Cold call opener (they answer, they're busy, you have ~8 seconds)

```
"[Name]? This is [you]. I'll be quick — are you in the middle of something?"

[If yes] "No problem, when's better — early morning or after five?"

[If no]
"I do follow-up for roofing companies. I filled in your form Tuesday morning as
a test and never heard back, which is normal and it's usually the cheapest thing
a shop can fix.

Real question though: the estimates you sent last month that didn't close —
what happens to those?"
```

Then **stop talking.** The answer is the whole call.

**What you'll hear, and what it means:**

| They say | Means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "We follow up." | Probe. | "How many times, typically?" *(If under 3, that's the opening.)* |
| "I don't know." | **Best case.** | "Most people don't. That's worth 15 minutes." |
| "They call us if they want it." | Classic. | "Some do. Top shops get 35–45% close rates by chasing four or five times. Most get 20–25%." |
| "Not interested." | Respect it. | "Fair enough — thanks for the straight answer." *Hang up.* |

## Voicemail (under 20 seconds, leave the number twice)

```
"[Name], [you] at [number]. I filled in your website form Tuesday and didn't
hear back — figured you'd want to know. Also had a question about how you handle
estimates that go quiet. [number] again. Thanks."
```

## The audit call

### Opening (2 min)
```
"Thanks for the time. Plan is: I ask you six numbers, we look at them together,
and I tell you honestly whether this is worth doing. If it isn't, I'll say so —
that's a real outcome, not a sales move. Fair?"
```

### The six numbers (5 min) — type them into the calculator live
1. Inbound calls in a typical month?
2. Roughly what share go unanswered?
3. Estimates sent per month?
4. Average estimate value?
5. Of those, what share close?
6. What do you pay for a lead right now?

### The reveal (5 min)
```
"So on your numbers: about [N] estimates a month don't close, worth roughly
[$X]. Industry data says a proper follow-up cadence recovers somewhere around
8% of those. For you that's about [$Y] a month.

Does that feel right, or is it high?"
```

**Let them argue it down.** A number they corrected is a number they believe.
Never defend an inflated figure.

### The mechanism (10 min)
Screen-share the dashboard. Hit these three, in order:

1. **"You write the messages."** Open Sequences, edit one live.
2. **"Nothing sends without you."** Open Approvals. "This is where they sit."
3. **"Here's the report."** Open the ROI report. Scroll to the unattributed
   section: *"These are jobs we chose not to claim credit for. That's the part
   I'd want to see if I were you."*

### The close (5 min)
```
"$1,500 a month. No setup fee, no contract, 30 days' notice.

First two weeks you're in review-only mode — the system writes every message but
sends nothing, so you can read it all first. Then you approve them one at a time
until you're comfortable. Automatic sending is a switch you flip, not me.

If after 60 days there's nothing in that report worth more than you paid, I'd
rather you cancelled.

Want to start Monday?"
```

### If they hesitate — the free audit fallback
```
"No pressure. Here's a smaller ask: send me last month's unsold estimates as a
spreadsheet. I'll load them in review-only mode and send you back exactly what
we'd have said to each one, and what they're worth. Nothing sends. Costs you
nothing.

If that's not compelling, we're done and I'll leave you alone."
```

## Onboarding call (30 min, first paying client)

Collect, in this order — everything maps to a field in the onboarding form:

1. Legal + trading name, timezone, business phone/email
2. Services offered (the list customers actually ask for)
3. Hours, and what happens after hours
4. How they book: phone, form, scheduling link?
5. Average job value, and their honest close rate
6. **Brand voice** — "Read me a text you'd send a customer." Copy their actual words.
7. Escalation: who gets an urgent lead, and how fast?
8. The five questions customers always ask (becomes the FAQ)
9. Where estimates live today, and can we get a CSV?
10. Review link

Then, in front of them: create the client, open Sequences, and read the first
missed-call message aloud in their voice. **Edit it with them on the call.**
That five minutes is what makes it feel like their system instead of yours.
