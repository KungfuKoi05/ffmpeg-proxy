# Your first 10 prospects — how to build the list in 90 minutes

**Owner:** Mercury

## Why this file contains a method and not ten company names

I could fill this page with ten real roofing companies and their phone numbers.
I'm not going to, for two reasons:

1. **I can't verify the signals from here.** The scoring model depends on
   whether they answer their phone, whether their reviews mention missed
   callbacks, and how fast they reply to a form. Every one of those requires
   someone to actually check. A list of real businesses annotated with invented
   claims like *"slow to respond"* would be worthless to you and unfair to them.
2. **Local beats clever.** Your first client should be within driving distance.
   You will close far more sitting in someone's office than on a call, and a
   local reference is worth more than a remote one.

What follows produces a better list than I could invent, in about 90 minutes.

---

## The 90-minute build

### Step 1 — pick the hunting ground (5 min)

Your metro, or the nearest one over ~250,000 people. Note it here:

**Target metro:** `________________`

### Step 2 — source 30 candidates (25 min)

Run these searches. Take companies ranked **4th–20th** in the map results, not
the top 3.

> The top 3 have marketing budgets, an office manager, and probably a
> ServiceTitan subscription. Positions 4–20 are the sweet spot: enough volume to
> have the problem, not enough staff to have solved it.

```
roofing contractors [metro]
roof replacement [metro]
hvac replacement [metro]
ac installation [metro]
home remodeling contractors [metro]
```

For each, record: business name, website, phone, Google rating, review count.

**Filter out immediately:**
- Under 20 reviews (too small to have the problem)
- Over 500 reviews (probably has staff for this)
- Any national franchise or private-equity roll-up (no local decision maker)
- Anyone whose site is a Facebook page only (too small)

You want roughly **30 survivors**.

### Step 3 — score from the outside (30 min, ~1 min each)

For each, check and record:

| Signal | Points | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| High-ticket trade | +20 | already true if you searched correctly |
| No web form | +12 | their homepage and contact page |
| No online booking | +8 | same |
| 50+ reviews | +10 | Google Maps |
| **Reviews mention no callback** | **+15** | **filter reviews to 1–3 stars and read them** |
| Runs paid ads | +10 | search `[their service] [city]`, look for Ads/LSA |
| Rating ≥ 4.5 | +5 | Google |
| Rating < 3.5 | −10 | Google |

**Read the bad reviews.** This is the highest-signal five minutes in the whole
process. Phrases like *"never called me back"*, *"waited two weeks for a quote"*,
*"had to chase them"* are customers publicly describing the exact problem you
solve — and they give you a specific, quotable opener.

### Step 4 — mystery-shop the top 10 (25 min)

Take the 10 highest scores and, **as a genuine prospective customer with a real
job in mind**:

1. Submit their web form (or call if there's no form). Note the exact timestamp.
2. Wait 48 hours.
3. Record whether anyone replied, and how long it took.

Add **+15** for slow or no response, and **+10** if the owner answered the phone
personally.

Be straight with them later about what you did and why. Never claim they ignored
you if they didn't — check your inbox and spam folder before you say it.

### Step 5 — load them into the pipeline (5 min)

Open `/sales` in the app, add each one with its signals ticked. The app scores
and tiers them. Work **Tier A first**.

## Warmer sources — do these first if you can

Cold outreach is the fallback. These convert several times better:

1. **Anyone you already know in the trades.** A roofer, an HVAC tech, a supplier
   rep. One warm introduction outperforms thirty cold emails.
2. **Supply houses.** The counter staff at a roofing or HVAC supplier know every
   contractor in the metro and who's growing. Buy them coffee.
3. **Your own contractors.** Whoever did work on your house. You are already a
   satisfied customer — that's a real relationship, not a cold call.
4. **Local trade associations / BNI chapters.** One meeting, ten owners in a
   room, all there specifically to do business.
5. **Facebook groups** for contractors in your state. Read for a week before
   posting anything. Answer questions. Never pitch cold.

## The bar for week one

**10 conversations, not 10 sales.** At 2–3 hours a day that's achievable, and
it's the only number that matters in the first week. Everything else —
the product, this repo, the roadmap — is already done and waiting.
