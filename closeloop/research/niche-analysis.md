# Niche analysis — which vertical Closeloop should sell to first

**Owner:** Atlas · **Date:** 2026-08-23 · **Status:** decided, revisit after 20 sales conversations

---

## The short version

**We are not starting with auto repair, and the product is not primarily a
missed-call texter.**

Two findings from the research below changed the plan:

1. **Missed-call text-back is a commodity.** It sells for $29–$479/month from a
   dozen established vendors. You cannot build a $1,500/month business on a
   feature a contractor can buy for $99.
2. **Average job value decides everything.** Recovering one auto repair job is
   worth ~$500. Recovering one HVAC replacement or roof is worth ~$8,000–$14,000.
   At a $1,500/month fee, the first requires ~3 extra jobs a month just to break
   even in the customer's mind; the second pays for five months of service with
   a single job.

**Decision: sell to high-ticket, estimate-driven residential trades — HVAC
replacement, roofing, and remodeling — and lead with unsold-estimate recovery,
with missed-call recovery as an included capability rather than the headline.**

- **Auto repair:** need ~3 recovered jobs/month to reach 1x. To feel like a
  bargain (5x) they need ~15 recovered jobs a month. Hard.
- **HVAC replacement / roofing:** **one** recovered job is 5–9x the monthly fee.
  Two a quarter and the client never questions the invoice.

### Why auto repair scored poorly

It is not a bad business — it is a bad *first* business for us:

- ARO of $428–$586 makes a $1,500/month fee a hard sell.
- Repair work is largely **unplanned and urgent**; the customer calls when the
  car breaks. There is much less of a considered "estimate that sits unsold for
  two weeks" — which is precisely the gap we are best at closing.
- Shop management systems (Tekmetric, Shopmonkey, Mitchell) already own the
  customer record and are adding messaging.

**Auto repair is a Phase-2 candidate at a lower price point ($400–600/month),
once the product runs itself.** Not first.

### Why roofing edged out HVAC

Roofing has the widest documented follow-up gap (84% no same-day follow-up), the
highest job values, and owners who are reachable. HVAC has better lead volume
and less seasonality but more competition from field-service platforms already
selling messaging add-ons.

**We will sell to both, leading with roofing, and treat remodeling as the
opportunistic third.** They share the same product; only the copy changes.

## What would make us abandon this

Atlas will call a pivot if, after **40 qualified conversations**:

- Fewer than 15% agree to a free audit → the message is wrong, or the pain isn't felt.
- Fewer than 3 of those audits convert to paid → the value isn't believed.
- Owners consistently say they already follow up estimates → the premise is wrong.
- Blended CAC exceeds ~$1,500 (one month's fee) with no path down.

**Most likely pivot if the premise holds but the vertical doesn't:** same
product, same code, aimed at commercial services with long sales cycles
(commercial HVAC/roofing, fire protection, industrial equipment) where estimate
values are higher still.

**Least likely pivot:** abandoning estimate follow-up. That gap is the most
robust finding in this document.

## Open questions for customer discovery

1. What actually happens to an unsold estimate today — CRM task, sticky note, or nothing?
2. Who would own the replies? Owner, office manager, or a sales rep?
3. Do they know their own estimate close rate? (If not, we can produce it — that alone may sell.)
4. What did they pay for the lead that produced the estimate? (Anchors our fee against LSA/ad spend.)
5. Which system holds their estimates, and can we read it?
