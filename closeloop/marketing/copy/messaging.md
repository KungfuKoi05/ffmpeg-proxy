# Core messaging

**Owner:** Nova

## The one-liner

> Closeloop follows up on the estimates and missed calls your team doesn't get
> to — and shows you exactly which jobs it brought back.

## The problem, in their language

> You quoted fourteen grand three weeks ago and never heard back. Nobody chased
> it. It's not on anyone's list. And you'll never know whether they went with
> someone else or just got busy.

## Headline options (tested by preference, not data yet)

1. **"Your best leads aren't lost. They're just never followed up."** ← in use
2. "84% of contractors never follow up an estimate the same day."
3. "The $14,000 quote you sent three weeks ago is still sitting there."

## Proof points — with sources, always

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Contractors not following up unsold estimates same-day | 84% | ustechautomations.com |
| Callers who never call back | 85% | dialzara.com |
| Contractor lines missing calls | 62% | ServiceTitan via contractorincharge.com |
| Close rate with a real follow-up system | 35–45% vs 20–25% | subcontractorhub.com |
| Average roof replacement | ~$9,607 | homeadvisor.com |

**Never state one of these without the source available.** A contractor who
Googles a stat and finds nothing stops believing everything else on the page.

## Words we don't use

`AI-powered` · `revolutionary` · `game-changing` · `seamless` · `unlock` ·
`10x` · `guaranteed results` · `set it and forget it`

That last one is worth calling out: it's the promise our competitors make and
the exact opposite of our positioning. We *want* the contractor reading their
messages.

## CTAs

- Primary: **"See how much revenue you're losing"** → calculator
- Secondary: **"Book a free revenue recovery audit"** → form
- In-report: **"Anyone you'd point us at?"** → referral

## Objection-preempting lines that earned their place on the site

> "Those are good tools and I'll happily name a few."
> (Disarms the $99-competitor comparison by refusing to pretend it doesn't exist.)

> "We don't have case studies yet."
> (Converts worse in week one, better in month three.)

> "If your average job is under about $2,000, we'll tell you honestly that the
> maths is harder."
> (Disqualifying in public makes every other claim more credible.)
