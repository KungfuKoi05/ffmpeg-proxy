Built for
rain

7 in. November’s average rainfall in Tacoma. July gets less than one.

Here a roof rarely fails in the sun. It fails at the edges, where water sits: a lifted shingle by the gutter, a chimney with tired flashing, moss packed into the gaps and holding water against the mat. We build every roof against that. Ice-and-water membrane along the eaves and in the valleys, synthetic underlayment over the whole deck, flashing bent on site for your chimney and walls, and enough attic ventilation to keep the decking dry from below. Then we book the tear-off for a run of dry days, so your house is never open to the sky in November. Repairs we do in any weather, usually the same week you call.

How a job runs

You will always know which stage we’re in. A leak repair usually goes straight from the inspection to the build, and is done the same week.

We walk the roof, go into the attic and look at the decking from below. You get photographs of everything we saw, good and bad, the same day.

Itemised and in writing: every material, the sheets of decking we expect to replace, and the price of each extra sheet if we find more rot once the old roof is off.

We book the tear-off for a run of dry days and watch the forecast with you. If rain moves in, we move the date. Your house is never left open overnight.

Questions we get

Can you replace a roof in winter?

We can, but we’d rather not. A tear-off leaves the deck open, so we book replacements for runs of dry days, which in Tacoma mostly means summer and early autumn. Repairs and storm tarps we do in any month.

Do I need a full tear-off, or can you roof over?

Code allows a second layer on some roofs, but we rarely advise it here. The old layer holds water, adds weight and hides rot in the decking. We’ll tell you what we see from the attic before you decide.

How long does a replacement take?

Most composition roofs take one to three dry days. Metal and cedar take longer. The estimator above shows the dry days for each size, and your written estimate gives the exact plan.

Is moss really a problem?

Yes. Moss holds water against the shingles and lifts their edges, which is how leaks start. We clear it by hand and fit zinc strips at the ridge. We never pressure-wash a roof; it strips the granules.

Will you work with my insurance?

We’ll photograph the damage and write an itemised estimate you can send to your insurer. The claim itself stays between you and them.

What warranty comes with a new roof?

The manufacturer’s warranty on materials, plus our own workmanship warranty. Its length and what it covers are written into your estimate before you sign.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. Washington requires every roofing contractor to be registered with Labor & Industries. Our registration number is at the bottom of this page, and we’ll show proof of insurance at the inspection.
