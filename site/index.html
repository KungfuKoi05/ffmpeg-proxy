The problem

Minutes are a statutory record. They have to be written, accurately, for every board meeting, for every association — and they can’t simply be skipped.

So a portfolio manager carrying eight to twelve associations writes them after hours, days later, from notes taken while also running the meeting. It’s the task that slips when the month gets busy, and the one boards notice when it’s late.

How it works

  1. Send the recording. Phone audio, Zoom, whatever the board already uses.
  2. We write the minutes. Attendance, motions, seconds, votes, and action items — in the association’s own format.
  3. You approve. Draft comes back within 48 hours. Tell us what to change, or send it to the board.
  4. The audio is deleted. We keep the document. The recording goes once the minutes are approved.

What comes back

  • Attendance, quorum, and call to order
  • Every motion — exact wording, who moved, who seconded, and the vote
  • Failed motions kept in the record, where a summary tool would drop them
  • Decisions recorded as decisions; discussion left out unless a motion turned on it
  • Executive session noted to your state’s standard, without the confidential detail
  • A separate action-item list: what, who, by when
  • Your template, your headings, your format

Written to your state’s rules, not a generic format

Minutes requirements aren’t uniform. Colorado is specific about how an executive session must appear in the record. Nevada treats the recording itself as part of the obligation. Florida and Arizona set retention periods and inspection deadlines. Georgia and North Carolina leave most of it to your governing documents.

We work to whichever applies to you — and where the statute is quiet, we work to your bylaws.

Common questions

Our boards don’t record meetings.

Most don’t yet. The chair notes at the top of the meeting that it’s being recorded for minutes, someone hits record on a phone, and the audio is deleted once minutes are approved. In two-party consent states like California and Florida, that announcement is what makes it lawful.

What about executive session?

Handled separately or excluded entirely — your call, confirmed in writing before we start. We sign an NDA before the first recording.

How long do you keep the recording?

Until you approve the minutes. Then it’s deleted, and we confirm that in writing. We keep the document, not the audio.

Who writes them?

AI does the first pass; a person reviews every set before it reaches you. We don’t send you anything a human hasn’t read.

Are you a law firm?

No. We prepare the document; we don’t certify the record or give legal advice. Your counsel remains your counsel.
