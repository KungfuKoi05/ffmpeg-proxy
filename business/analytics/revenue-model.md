# $10,000/Month Revenue Model

**Owner:** CFO | All figures `[ESTIMATE]` or `[ASSUMPTION]` until E-001 reads out.

---

## The shape

**8 customers × ~$1,250/month = $10,000 MRR**, at $79/meeting and ~16 meetings per customer.

Chosen over the alternatives because it fits the binding constraint. 100 customers at $100 would need
100 sales conversations and 100 onboardings — impossible in 6–10 hours a week. Deal size is not
vanity here; it is the only way the arithmetic closes.

## The ladder

### $0 → $1,000 · one customer · weeks 1–5

80 hand-sent emails → ~6 replies → ~3 free samples → 1 paid customer at ~13 meetings/month.

Delivery is fully manual and timed at every step. Gross margin is roughly 3% and that is accepted —
the first customer is bought as information, not profit. What it produces: a real reply rate, a real
delivery time, a real template, and one reference.

**Founder hours: ~8/week**, nearly all outreach.

### $1,000 → $3,000 · three customers · weeks 5–9

160 more sends, plus the first referral. Transcription and template assembly get scripted once five
manual deliveries have shown where the time actually goes.

Delivery drops to ~45 minutes. Margin reaches ~51%. **Founder hours: 8–10/week**, now split between
outreach and delivery — the first real capacity squeeze.

### $3,000 → $5,000 · five customers · weeks 9–13

Referrals begin to outperform cold outreach; CAM owners are densely networked and a happy customer in
Atlanta is worth more than forty cold emails. Expansion offers (action-item tracking, violation
letters) raise revenue per customer without new acquisition cost.

Delivery ~20 minutes at the tuned stage. Margin ~77%. **Founder hours: 8–10/week.**

### $5,000 → $10,000 · eight customers · weeks 13–20

**This rung has a hard gate.** 130 meetings a month at 20 minutes each is 43 hours — above the
founder's ceiling. One of two things must happen, both funded by revenue that exists by then:

- **Hire a contractor for QA** at ~$20/hr. 130 meetings × 20 min = 43 hrs ≈ $870/month against
  $10,000 revenue. Margin stays above 80% and founder hours fall to under 5.
- **Or automate further**, cutting QA to ~10 minutes and keeping it in-house at ~22 hrs/month.

The contractor path is recommended: it is cheaper than the founder's time, reversible, and reaches
the actual goal — under 5 hours a week — faster than more engineering would.

**Founder hours at $10K: under 5/week.** If that number is rising rather than falling as revenue
grows, the business is failing at its purpose regardless of the revenue line.

## Sensitivity

| Scenario | Customers needed | Timeline | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base (8% reply, 40% close, 16 mtgs) | 8 | ~16 weeks | Works |
| Half the reply rate | 8 | ~32 weeks | Slow but survivable |
| Half the meetings per customer | 16 | ~32 weeks | **Breaks** — 16 onboardings will not fit |
| Price at $120/meeting | 5 | ~11 weeks | **Best case — test this early** |
| 20% monthly churn | never | never | **Fatal** — refilling a bucket |

Two lessons the table makes obvious. **Price is the strongest lever** — at $120 the target needs five
customers instead of eight and arrives five weeks sooner, which is why E-004 should run as soon as
there are ten conversations to test it on. And **churn is the only fatal variable**: every other bad
case is slow, that one is terminal.

## What is deliberately not in this model

No paid advertising. No purchased leads. No hires before $5K MRR. No infrastructure spend. No
assumption that a referral engine appears on its own — it is modeled as starting at customer three
and only after a customer has been asked directly.

Capital required to execute this entire ladder: **$0.** The $100 stays intact until revenue exists.
