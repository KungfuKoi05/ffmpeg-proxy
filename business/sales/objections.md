# Objection Handling

The principle: **never argue with the objection.** Ask the question underneath it. Most objections in
this market are not disagreements — they are the fastest way for a busy owner to end a conversation.

---

### "Our boards don't record meetings."

*The one that decides the business. Expect it most often.*

> Most don't yet. It's a two-minute change — the chair says at the top that the meeting is being
> recorded for minutes, someone hits record on a phone, and the audio is deleted once the minutes are
> approved. The software vendors in this space do exactly the same thing.
>
> Would one of your boards be open to trying it once?

**If they say no board will ever record:** thank them, mark it, and move on. **Log this reason
specifically** — if half of respondents say this, D-001's failure criteria are met and the business
gets killed. This objection is the experiment.

---

### "We already use [BoardBreeze / MinuteSmith / our management software]."

> Makes sense — how much time does a manager still spend on a set of minutes after the software has
> done its part?

Their answer is the entire sale. The software transcribes and formats; a human still reviews,
corrects names and motions, chases what was unclear, and formats to the association's template. If
that is still 45–90 minutes, we replace it. If they say five minutes, they are genuinely served —
mark `closed_lost`, and note it, because that is important evidence about the market.

**Never disparage the incumbent.** They chose it.

---

### "Our managers are supposed to do this. It's in their job description."

> It is — and it's usually the part that slips when they're busy, and the part they mention when
> they resign. This isn't about whether they can. It's about what you'd rather they spend the evening
> on.

---

### "How do I know the minutes will be accurate?"

> You don't, yet. That's why the first two are free — you check them against the meeting you were in.
> And if a set ever comes back wrong, you don't pay for it.
>
> One thing worth knowing: minutes should record what was *decided*, not everything that was *said*.
> We follow the association's template and keep discussion out unless a motion turned on it.

---

### "What about confidentiality? Boards discuss delinquencies and legal matters."

*Take this one seriously — it is the most legitimate objection in the list.*

> Fair. Three things: we sign an NDA before the first recording. Executive session is handled
> separately or excluded entirely, your call. And the audio is deleted once you approve the minutes —
> we keep the document, not the recording.

If they need something more formal, that is a **founder decision**, not an agent one. Queue it in
`APPROVALS.md`.

---

### "What's the catch on 'free'?"

> No catch and no contract. Two meetings, you keep the minutes either way. If you want a third,
> it's $79. If you don't, we're done — there's nothing to cancel.

---

### "Send me some pricing and I'll take a look."

*Usually a polite exit. Do not send a deck.*

> $79 a meeting, billed monthly on what you actually use, no minimum. But pricing won't tell you
> anything the sample will — forward me one recording and you'll know in 48 hours whether this is
> worth a conversation.

---

### "We're too small / too big."

**Too small (under 15 associations):** genuinely a poor fit. Say so, and ask who else in their
market has this problem. Honesty here buys referrals — and in this industry, owners talk.

**Too big (national brand):** expect procurement and vendor onboarding. Ask who owns the minutes
process at the regional level and whether a single-office pilot is possible.

---

### "Call me next quarter."

> Happy to. Anything specific changing then — a manager leaving, budget season, an audit?

Their answer is the real timeline. Set the follow-up for the date they name, note the trigger, and
actually do it. Log as `nurture`.

---

## What is never said

- No invented customers, logos, testimonials, or "we work with firms like yours"
- No claimed accuracy percentage we have not measured
- No implied legal or compliance guarantee — we produce a document, we do not certify a record
- No pressure, no fake scarcity, no fabricated deadline
