## The chain

Every decision runs through this, and a break anywhere kills the business.

**Problem** — Minutes are a statutory record for every board meeting of every association. They
cannot be skipped. Today they are written after hours, days later, by a portfolio manager carrying
8–12 associations who is already over capacity
[FACT: https://accuinc.com/how-many-hoas-does-your-community-manager-manage/],
[FACT: https://managecasa.com/articles/state-of-hoa-community-association-management].

**Customer** — The management company, not the association. 8,000–9,000 firms manage 60–70% of
~377,000 US associations [FACT: https://www.avidxchange.com/blog/2026-community-manager-trends/].
One firm is 25–50 meetings a month; one association is one.

**Offer** — Send the recording, get board-ready minutes in 48 hours. $79/meeting. First two free.

**Acquisition** — Hand-sent personalized email to named owners, then referrals. $0. The offer leads
with completed work rather than a request for time, which is the only lever available with no budget,
no case studies, and no logo.

**Fulfillment** — Audio extraction (already built, free), transcription, drafting to the
association's template, human QA, delivery. Manual first, deliberately.

**Retention** — The template library. After six months of an association's minutes in their format,
switching means retraining someone on fifty formats. Retention comes from accumulated context, not
from a contract.

**Scale** — Expansion offers to the same buyer (action items, violation letters, board packets), then
the identical delivery engine pointed at municipal boards (OPP-09, already scored 73.5).

**The weakest link is Fulfillment**, at 90 manual minutes against a $79 price. That is a 3% gross
margin on the first deliveries, and it is knowingly accepted: the first deliveries buy information,
not profit. If it does not fall to 45 minutes by customer three, the price rises or the business dies.
That trigger is written into `analytics/unit-economics.md` so it is a decision, not a slow bleed.

## Why this and not the others

Twenty-five opportunities, ten factors each. The three finalists were separated less by demand than
by fit with our actual constraints.

The healthcare cluster had the strongest ROI story in the entire study — dental verification replaces
a ~$3,600/month role and sells for $300–800/month
[FACT: https://www.cecomputech.com/guides/dental-billing-services-costs/]. We killed it anyway,
because it needs PHI handling and the customer's own portal credentials. **The best problem is not
the same thing as the best problem for us, right now, with $0 and no legal review.**

Order entry and medical chronology were killed by timing: both are real, both are large, and both are
already owned by funded competitors, with chronology prices already down to $28
[FACT: https://www.tavrn.ai/blog/medical-chronology-software]. Arriving late to a commoditizing
market with no capital is not contrarian, it is just losing.

Certified payroll (81.5) is the closest call and remains the designated fallback. Its managed tier is
a proven $1,000–5,000/month purchase [FACT: https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/certified-payroll-services].
It lost on cadence and consequence: weekly filings where an error withholds a contractor's payment
are the wrong place to learn a business in 6–10 hours a week.

OPP-08 won on one thing the others could not match: **we can deliver a complete, genuinely useful,
free sample within 24 hours at zero cost.** With no capital and no reputation, the finished work is
the only marketing asset available. Every other finalist required a meeting, credentials, or
integration before the buyer could see anything.

## What would make me kill this

Written down in advance, in `DECISION_LOG.md` D-001:

- ≥50% of respondents say no board will record → the model is broken, not the message
- <2 replies from 40, twice, after a message revision
- 0 paid engagements after 80 sends
- Manual delivery over 90 minutes with no automation path

Any one of these promotes OPP-01 the same week. The research is done; there is no restart cost.

## What I am watching

1. **E-002** — do boards record? Nothing else matters if the answer is no
2. **Reply rate** — below 5%, the founder's hours cannot generate enough conversations
3. **Minutes per delivery** — the number that decides business versus job
4. **Who replies** — if only Tier-C nationals respond, the ICP is wrong and deal size collapses

## Standing judgment

Do not build anything before someone has said yes. Do not raise the price before it has been
rejected. Do not automate a process that has not been performed by hand ten times. And do not let a
pending approval idle the company — there is always work that is not blocked.
