## The finding that changes the business: Nevada

**NRS 116.31083 appears to make audio recording of executive board meetings mandatory.**

The statute reportedly provides that the secretary "shall cause each meeting of the executive board
to be audio recorded and the minutes to be recorded or otherwise taken at each meeting," that a
board "meeting in executive session … must not be audio recorded," and that not more than **30 days**
after each meeting the association must make available the **audio recording, the minutes, and a
summary of the minutes**. Minutes are maintained "until the common-interest community is
terminated" — effectively permanent retention
[FACT: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-116/statute-116-31083/].

**Why this matters more than anything else in this file.**

The single largest risk to this business, written into `DECISION_LOG.md` D-001 as a kill criterion,
is *"≥50% of respondents say their boards do not record meetings → the delivery model is broken."*
Experiment E-002 exists solely to measure it.

If this reading is correct, **that risk does not exist in Nevada.** The recording we need is not
something we have to persuade a board to start producing — it is something they are already legally
obliged to produce, and to hand to any owner who asks within 30 days.

Two further consequences:

1. **Nevada should lead the outreach, not sit at position 1, 2, and 20 by accident.** Three batch-1
   prospects are Nevada: Desert Living, SMG, and Las Vegas Community Management Group.
2. **The statutory "summary of the minutes" is a second deliverable** the association must produce
   alongside the minutes themselves. Generic transcription tools do not produce it. We can, at no
   extra cost, and it is a natural upsell or differentiator.

**Do not put any of this in an email until the statute is read directly.** See the warning above.

## Florida

- Minutes of all association and board meetings are **official records** the association must
  maintain, under Chapter 720 (§720.303)
  [FACT: https://minutesmith.com/blog/florida-hoa-meeting-minutes-requirements]
- Must capture quorum, motions, votes, and action items
- **Retention: 7 years**
- **Inspection: within 10 business days** of a written member request
- **Two-party consent state** — a board recording its own meeting announces it at the top
  [FACT: https://thehoahandbook.com/is-it-legal-to-record-hoa-meetings-state-laws-explained/]

**Prospects:** Ocean Blue Community Management (Tampa), Wise Property Management (St. Petersburg)

## Arizona

- The Planned Community Act and Condominium Act require associations to maintain minutes and make
  them available for member inspection
  [FACT: https://minutesmith.com/blog/hoa-minutes-requirements-by-state]
- Should document quorum, motions, votes, and approved expenditures
- **Retention: 7 years** · **Inspection: within 10 business days**

**Prospects:** Heywood Community Management, National Property Services, City Property Management

## Colorado

- CCIOA requires the association to keep **permanent records** of executive board meeting minutes
  [FACT: https://dre.colorado.gov/hoa-meetings]
- **§38-33.3-308(7) is the only CCIOA section addressing minutes content:** minutes of any meeting at
  which an executive session was held must **indicate that an executive session was held and state
  its general subject matter**. The worked example given by the state: *"At 8:15 p.m. the board went
  into executive session to discuss a covenant enforcement lawsuit with the Association's attorney."*
  [FACT: https://dre.colorado.gov/hoa-meetings]
- No statutory retention period; 7 years is the accepted practice
- §38-33.3-209.4 gives every owner a right to inspect books and records

**Prospects:** ACCU Inc (Denver), CAP Management (Denver)

## Georgia

Georgia's HOA statute requires associations to maintain meeting records and make them available to
members, but is **substantially less prescriptive** than Florida or Colorado. The association's own
governing documents control the detail
[FACT: https://minutesmith.com/blog/hoa-minutes-requirements-by-state].

**Practical consequence:** ask for the association's bylaws, not the statute. Five of twenty
prospects are Georgia — the largest single state in batch 1 — so the intake question "what do your
bylaws require?" matters more here than anywhere else.

**Prospects:** All-In-One, Douglas Property Management, HOA Community Management, Homeside
Properties, Atlanta Community Services, Access Management Group, Silverleaf

## North Carolina

Also **less prescriptive** than Florida or Colorado; many NC bylaws impose record requirements
beyond the statute [FACT: https://minutesmith.com/blog/hoa-minutes-requirements-by-state].
Same practical consequence as Georgia — the bylaws are the specification.

**Prospects:** Association Management Solutions, Cusick Company, Association Management Group

## Universal drafting standard

Independent of state, the professional standard is consistent, and it is the core of the product:

**Minutes record decisions, not discussion.**

The most common failure is writing something closer to a transcript. When members see their remarks
written out they want to edit them, and the recorder becomes so busy capturing quotes that motions
get missed [FACT: https://www.hoamanagement.com/hoa-meeting-minutes-mistakes/].

| Include | Exclude |
|---|---|
| Motions: exact wording, mover, seconder, vote outcome | Verbatim discussion |
| **Failed motions** — they prove the board considered and rejected something | Personal opinions, accusations, arguments |
| Dissenting votes and abstentions | Editorial characterization of anyone's conduct |
| Reports: presenter and topic only | Full summaries of reports |
| Attendance, quorum, call to order, adjournment | Anything shaping a narrative |

Drafting within **24–48 hours** of the meeting is the stated best practice
[FACT: https://www.hoamanagement.com/hoa-meeting-minutes-mistakes/] — which is precisely the window
our 48-hour turnaround occupies, and a reason the offer is credible rather than merely fast.
