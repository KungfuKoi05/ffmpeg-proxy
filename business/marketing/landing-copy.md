## The problem

Minutes are a statutory record. They have to be written, accurately, for every board meeting, for
every association.

So a portfolio manager carrying 8–12 associations writes them after hours, days later, from notes
made while also running the meeting. It's the task that slips when the month gets busy, and it's the
one boards notice when it's late.

---

## How it works

**1. Send the recording.** Phone audio, Zoom, whatever the board already uses. Forward the file.

**2. We write the minutes.** Attendance, motions, seconds, votes, and action items — in the
association's own format.

**3. You approve.** Draft comes back within 48 hours. Tell us what to change, or send it to the board.

**4. The audio is deleted.** We keep the document. The recording goes once the minutes are approved.

## What comes back

- Attendance, quorum, and call to order
- Every motion, who moved, who seconded, and the vote
- Decisions recorded as decisions — discussion left out unless a motion turned on it
- A separate action-item list: what, who, by when
- Your template, your headings, your format

## Common questions

**Our boards don't record meetings.**
Most don't yet. The chair notes at the top of the meeting that it's being recorded for minutes,
someone hits record on a phone, and the audio is deleted once minutes are approved. In two-party
consent states like California and Florida, that announcement is what makes it lawful.

**What about executive session?**
Handled separately or excluded entirely — your call. We sign an NDA before the first recording.

**How long do you keep the recording?**
Until you approve the minutes. Then it's deleted. We keep the document, not the audio.

**Who writes them?**
AI does the first pass; a human reviews every set before it reaches you. We don't send you anything
we haven't read.
