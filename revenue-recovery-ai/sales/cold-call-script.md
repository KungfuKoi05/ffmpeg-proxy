# Cold call script

**Opener**
"Hi, is this the owner? I'll be quick — I work with HVAC companies around
[city]. Can I ask you one question and then get off the phone?"

**The question**
"When your team is on a job and the phone rings, what happens to that call?"

Let them answer. Most say voicemail, an answering service, or "we call them
back." All three are the opening.

**The problem**
"Right. The thing that gets expensive is the ones who don't leave a voicemail —
they just dial the next company. You never find out it happened, so it never
shows up anywhere in your numbers."

**What we do**
"We put an assistant on your line that picks up when nobody can. It asks what's
wrong, gets their name and address, and books them into your calendar. If it's
not sure about something, it hands off to you instead of guessing. It never
quotes a price."

**The ask**
"Worth fifteen minutes to see it answer a call? I'll call your number and you
listen to it work."

## Objections

**"We have an answering service."**
"How many of those messages turn into booked jobs? Most take a message. This one
books the appointment while the customer is still on the phone — that's the
difference."

**"I don't want a robot talking to my customers."**
"Fair. Two things: it says it's an assistant if asked, and it hands off to a
human the moment it's unsure. Listen to a call and tell me if you'd let it near
your customers. If not, no hard feelings."

**"How much?"**
"$499 a month. One recovered repair covers it. If it doesn't recover one, you
shouldn't keep paying for it."

**"Send me some information."**
"I can, but it's an assistant that answers a phone — it's better heard than
read. Two minutes on a live call right now?"

**"Not interested."**
"Understood. Can I ask — is it that you're not missing calls, or that you've
already got it handled? If you're genuinely not missing any, I'll leave you
alone."
