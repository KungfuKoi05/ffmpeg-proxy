# Objection handling

**"It'll sound like a robot and my customers will hate it."**
It speaks in one or two short sentences and asks one thing at a time. It says
it's an assistant when asked. Offer to let them hear it before answering.

**"What if it books someone we can't get to?"**
It only offers slots from the availability they configure, and re-checks for
conflicts before booking. If the slot went in the meantime, it says so and
offers another rather than double-booking.

**"What if it says something wrong?"**
It can only assert what's in their knowledge base. Prices, availability and
policies are forbidden — those escalate. Every conversation is reviewed
afterward and flagged if it looks like it invented something. They can read the
full transcript of every call.

**"We already have an answering service."**
Ask what percentage of those messages become booked jobs. An answering service
takes a message; this books the appointment during the call.

**"My office manager handles it."**
Then this is for nights, weekends and the times she's on the other line. It's
not a replacement, it's the overflow.

**"Too expensive."**
Ask their average repair ticket. If it's $750, one recovered job in a month
covers it. If they genuinely aren't missing calls, they shouldn't buy it —
say so.

**"Is it locking me in?"**
Monthly, cancel in the billing portal.

**"What about my number?"**
Unchanged. They forward their existing line. Nothing on the trucks or website
changes.

**"What happens in a real emergency?"**
Gas smell, CO alarm, smoke, or a medical emergency: it tells the caller to hang
up and call emergency services, then alerts the owner immediately. That
behaviour isn't configurable.
