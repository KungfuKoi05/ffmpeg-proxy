# Cold email

Rules: short, one question, no statistics you cannot source, no fake urgency.

## Email 1

**Subject:** missed calls at {{company}}

{{first_name}} — quick question.

When your techs are on a job and the phone rings, where does that call go?

Most shops I talk to say voicemail. The expensive ones are the callers who don't
leave a message and just dial the next company — you never find out it happened.

We put an assistant on your line that answers when nobody can, gets the details,
and books the job. It never quotes a price and hands off to a human when it's
unsure.

Worth two minutes to hear it answer a call?

— {{sender_name}}

## Email 2 (day 4)

**Subject:** re: missed calls at {{company}}

One thing I should have said: your number doesn't change. You forward your
existing line to it, so nothing on your trucks or your website changes.

Still worth two minutes?

## Email 3 (day 9)

**Subject:** the after-hours version

The part owners usually care about most is after-hours. A no-cooling call at
8pm in July either gets answered or goes to whoever answers first.

If you're already covering nights, ignore me. If it's going to voicemail, that's
the one I'd want to fix.

## Breakup (day 16)

**Subject:** closing the loop

I'll stop here — I don't want to be the guy filling up your inbox.

If missed calls ever become the thing you want to fix, reply to this and I'll
pick it back up.
